# Clinic Spanish Course — content spec (read fully before writing)

## Learner
A US cardiac electrophysiologist (Chicago). Intermediate: good basic vocabulary, comfortable in the PRESENT tense, weak in past tenses and in building sentences. Goal: conversational Spanish + medical Spanish with Latin American patients (mostly Mexican / Central American).

## Register and language rules (non-negotiable)
- Latin American Spanish. Mexican-leaning everyday vocabulary that is widely understood (celular, cobija, carro/coche ok, manejar, cuadras, la presión, el azúcar for blood sugar colloquially, "¿mande?" is fine to recognize).
- NO vosotros anywhere. Plural "you" = ustedes.
- Medical track: the clinician ALWAYS uses **usted** with patients. Patients may use usted with the doctor.
- Conversation track: tú among friends/coworkers/family; usted with strangers/elders. Make the register match the situation in every item.
- Natural spoken phrasing, not textbook. In speech, ir a + infinitive is the normal future; preterite (not present perfect) for finished events today/yesterday; present perfect for "ever / yet / lately".
- Perfect accents and punctuation: á é í ó ú ñ ü, opening ¿ and ¡. Accents on interrogatives (qué, cómo, dónde, cuándo, cuánto, por qué) and on commands with attached pronouns (acuéstese, dígame).
- English translations: natural English, faithful meaning.
- Medical accuracy: content must be clinically correct and generic. Do NOT state institution- or drug-specific numbers that vary in practice (lifting limits in pounds, exact days of restriction, INR targets, doses). Use phrasings like "por unos días", "lo que le indique su médico", "por lo general". Symptoms, warning signs and anatomy must be correct.
- Every item must have exactly ONE defensible correct answer. If Spanish allows several (word order, synonyms, with/without subject pronoun, usted/él ambiguity), either list all acceptable answers or rewrite the item so only one fits.
- No ambiguity traps: a fill-in must be answerable from the sentence + hint. If tense is not given in the hint, the context (time words like "ayer", "mañana", "todos los días", "cuando era niño") must force exactly one tense.
- Grammar scope: an item may only require grammar taught in this level or earlier levels of the SAME track (see outline). Medical levels state a "best after" conversation level; their items may use grammar up to that conversation level.

## Levels
Conversation track (id C1..C8):
- C1 Building sentences in the present: word order, question words, negation (no, nunca, nada, nadie), connectors (y, pero, porque, entonces, también, tampoco), gustar/doler/encantar/importar pattern, stem-changing presents, tener que / poder / querer + infinitive.
- C2 Describing: ser vs estar, hay, tener expressions (hambre, frío, años, miedo), adjective agreement, possessives, comparisons (más/menos…que, tan…como, mejor/peor), muy/mucho/poco.
- C3 What happened: preterite regular + key irregulars (ser/ir, tener, estar, hacer, poder, decir, dar, ver, poner, venir, querer, saber), spelling changes (llegué, busqué, empecé), time markers (ayer, anoche, la semana pasada, hace dos días), sequencing (primero, después, luego, al final).
- C4 Telling a story: imperfect (regular + ser/ir/ver), preterite vs imperfect (background vs event; "used to"), mientras, cuando, de niño, siempre/a veces/todos los días; meaning-change preterites (supe, conocí, quise/no quise, pude).
- C5 Plans and recent past: ir a + infinitive, simple future (also for probability), present perfect (ya, todavía no, alguna vez, nunca, últimamente), acabar de + infinitive, desde hace / hace … que / llevar + time.
- C6 Pronouns and ongoing actions: direct/indirect object pronouns, double pronouns (se lo, me la), reflexive verbs and daily routine, pronoun placement with infinitives, gerunds and commands, estar + gerund (progressive), seguir + gerund.
- C7 Wanting, needing, advising: present subjunctive (regular + key irregulars: ser, estar, ir, haber, saber, dar), triggers (quiero que, necesito que, es importante que, ojalá, espero que, me alegra que, dudo que, no creo que), cuando/para que/antes de que + subjunctive vs indicative for habits, affirmative/negative tú and usted commands with pronouns.
- C8 Hypotheticals and smooth conversation: conditional (polite requests, advice: "yo en su lugar…"), imperfect subjunctive (-ra), si + imperfect subjunctive + conditional vs si + present, como si, conversational glue (pues, bueno, o sea, la verdad, ¿verdad?, ¿no?, a ver, oye/oiga, perdón ¿cómo dijo?), asking to repeat/slow down, opinions (me parece que, creo que, no creo que + subj).

Medical track (id M1..M8) — clinician speaking with Spanish-speaking patients and families:
- M1 Opening the visit (best after: C1): greetings by time of day, introducing yourself and role, asking name/date of birth, checking understanding, offering an interpreter, asking permission to examine, body parts (head to toe incl. pecho, brazo izquierdo, cuello, mandíbula, espalda, estómago, piernas, tobillos), basic present-tense symptom questions (¿Le duele…? ¿Tiene…?), pain scale 0–10, numbers, days/times.
- M2 The story of the symptom (best after: C3): onset, duration (desde hace, hace cuánto, ¿cuándo empezó?), location, character (opresión, presión, ardor, punzada, como un peso, apretado), radiation (se corre, se va al brazo/la mandíbula), severity, timing (constante, va y viene), triggers and relief (al caminar, en reposo, con el esfuerzo, al acostarse), associated symptoms; preterite/imperfect in history taking.
- M3 Cardiac review of symptoms (best after: C4): chest pain, shortness of breath on exertion, orthopnea (¿cuántas almohadas?), waking up short of breath, leg swelling, palpitations (lo siente rápido / fuerte / irregular / brincos), dizziness, near-fainting and fainting (what were you doing, warning signs before, how long, injuries, witnessed), fatigue, exercise tolerance (cuadras, escaleras, pisos), claudication.
- M4 Medicines and history (best after: C5): medication list and adherence (¿Se le olvida…?), how and when taken, anticoagulants and bleeding questions, allergies vs side effects, past medical history (presión alta, diabetes/azúcar alta, colesterol, infarto, embolia/derrame cerebral, apnea del sueño, tiroides), surgeries/procedures, family history (muerte súbita, problemas del corazón), social history (fuma, alcohol, drogas, ejercicio, trabajo, con quién vive).
- M5 Exam and tests (best after: C6): usted commands with pronouns for the exam (siéntese, acuéstese, respire hondo, aguante la respiración, desabróchese / quítese la camisa, póngase la bata, vuélvase del lado izquierdo, relájese), vital signs, ECG, echocardiogram, stress test (caminadora), Holter / heart monitor (parche), blood tests, preparing the patient ("le voy a poner…", "va a sentir…").
- M6 Explaining the diagnosis and options (best after: C7): atrial fibrillation (el corazón late irregular/desordenado), flutter, SVT, slow heart rhythm / bloqueo, heart failure (el corazón no bombea con fuerza), stroke risk and why anticoagulation, rate vs rhythm control, medication vs ablation; checking understanding (teach-back), shared decision making, answering questions; subjunctive for recommendations.
- M7 Procedures and consent (best after: C7): ablation, cardioversion, pacemaker, defibrillator (desfibrilador/DAI), heart catheterization; preparation (en ayunas, which medicines to hold — generic, "le vamos a decir cuáles"), what happens on the day (sedación/anestesia, catéteres por la ingle/la vena de la pierna), how long, risks in plain words (sangrado, moretón, infección, coágulos, daño a un vaso, perforación, necesidad de otro procedimiento), benefits/alternatives, consent signature, family questions.
- M8 After the procedure and follow-up (best after: C8): groin/wound care (no se moje la herida, no levante cosas pesadas — no specific pounds), arm restrictions after device, warning signs and when to call vs go to the ER (sangrado que no para, hinchazón, fiebre, dolor de pecho, falta de aire, desmayo), follow-up visits, remote monitoring, driving and returning to work (generic), medicines after the procedure, phone call follow-ups, reassurance and empathy phrases.

```json
{

  "title": "What happened",   // short English title

  "grammar": [                // 3–5 blocks
    {"h": "Regular preterite endings", "p": "Short plain-English explanation. Use *asterisks* for Spanish words.", "ex": [["Ayer caminé una hora.", "Yesterday I walked for an hour."]]}
```

### Exercise pool (`items`) — exact counts per level
- 20 × `mc`, 20 × `fill`, 16 × `tiles`, 10 × `listen`, 6 × `dlg`.
- Give every item a unique `"id"`: `"<LEVEL>-<type>-<nn>"`, e.g. `"C3-fill-07"`.
- Mix difficulty: about one third easy, one third medium, one third hard. Hard items should require the level's core skill (e.g. choosing the tense) not obscure vocabulary.

`mc` — multiple choice, 4 options, exactly one correct:
```json
{"id":"C3-mc-01","t":"mc","q":"Ayer ___ al doctor.","qen":"Yesterday I went to the doctor.","opts":["voy","fui","iba","iré"],"a":1,"why":"A completed event yesterday → preterite: fui."}
```
`q` may be a Spanish sentence with ___, a question in English ("How do you say …?"), or a situation ("A patient says … — best reply?"). `qen` optional English gloss. Distractors must be plausible but clearly wrong. Vary the position of the correct answer.

`fill` — type the missing word(s):
```json
{"id":"C3-fill-01","t":"fill","q":"¿Qué ___ usted anoche?","en":"What did you eat last night?","hint":"comer","a":["comió"],"why":"usted + preterite → comió."}
```
`a` lists every acceptable answer (accents matter; the app also tells the learner when only accents are wrong). Blank = one word or a short verb phrase (max 3 words). `hint` = infinitive or a short clue; never the answer itself.

`tiles` — learner taps words to build the Spanish sentence:
```json
{"id":"C3-tiles-01","t":"tiles","en":"What did the doctor tell you?","a":"¿Qué le dijo el doctor?","alt":[],"extra":["dice","lo"]}
```
`a` is the target (4–11 words, normal punctuation). The app splits `a` on spaces and strips punctuation for the tiles, so every word must be its own token. `alt` = other fully correct orderings of the SAME words (written as full sentences). Spanish word order is flexible, so check: could the subject go before or after the verb? Could an adverb or time phrase move ("Ayer fui…" / "Fui… ayer")? List every natural reordering, or prefer sentences whose order is fixed. `extra` = 2–3 distractor words (wrong conjugation, wrong pronoun, wrong preposition) that must NOT fit anywhere in a correct sentence.

`listen` — the app reads `say` aloud (Spanish text-to-speech); learner picks the meaning:
```json
{"id":"C3-listen-01","t":"listen","say":"Me desmayé en el baño.","opts":["I fainted in the bathroom.","I get dizzy in the bathroom.","I fell in the shower.","I fainted at the bank."],"a":0}
```
Options in English; distractors should test listening (tense, person, similar-sounding words).

`dlg` — scripted mini-conversation; learner chooses replies:
```json
{"id":"C3-dlg-01","t":"dlg","title":"Catching up with a coworker","turns":[
  {"who":"Ana","es":"¡Hola! ¿Qué hiciste el fin de semana?","en":"Hi! What did you do on the weekend?"},
  {"you":true,"opts":["Fui a la playa con mi familia.","Voy a la playa con mi familia.","Iba a la playa mañana."],"a":0,"why":"She asks about the past weekend → preterite."},
  {"who":"Ana","es":"¡Qué bien! ¿Y qué tal el clima?","en":"Great! And how was the weather?"},
  {"you":true,"opts":["Hace mucho calor mañana.","Hizo mucho calor.","Haga calor."],"a":1,"why":"…"}
]}
```
4–7 turns, 2–3 of them `you` turns with 3 options each (Spanish), exactly one correct. Wrong options: wrong tense/person/register or a reply that doesn't fit. Medical dlg: the "who" is the patient or family member (e.g. "Paciente", "Sra. López", "Hija"); `you` is the clinician.

### Roleplay (`roleplay`) — 2 per level, practice with an AI playing the other person
```json
{"title":"Chest pain in clinic","setup":"English, 1–2 sentences: who you are, who they are, what to find out.","persona":"English instructions for the AI: who it plays (name, age, origin), background facts it knows, how much Spanish complexity to use (match this level), what to reveal only when asked.","opening":"First line the AI character says, in Spanish.","goals":["Find out when the pain started","Ask what makes it worse","…"]}
```
3–5 concrete goals. Persona facts must be medically coherent.

## Quality bar
Before saving, re-read every Spanish string for: accents, ¿¡, agreement (gender/number), usted vs tú consistency, verb form/tense, natural phrasing, and that exactly one option/answer is correct. Then run the JSON validation and a count check (20/20/16/10/6 items; 35–45 vocab; 2 roleplays).
